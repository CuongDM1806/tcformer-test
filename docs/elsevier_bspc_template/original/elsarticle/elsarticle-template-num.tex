\begin{abstract}
Cross-subject motor-imagery electroencephalography (MI-EEG) decoding is limited by low signal-to-noise ratios, scarce labeled data, and strong distribution shifts across subjects and sessions. We propose a domain-adaptive selective state-space framework for this setting. A multi-kernel temporal convolutional front-end with depthwise spatial filtering and channel-group attention extracts local spectral--spatial features. Five selective state-space blocks with alternating forward and backward scans then model long-range temporal context at a cost linear in sequence length, and a convolutional shortcut with a grouped dilated temporal convolutional network fuses local and global information. To reduce cross-subject shift, the framework combines per-subject Riemannian alignment, an automatic domain alignment (AutoDIAL) layer that learns how strongly target features are normalized with target rather than source statistics, gradient-reversal adversarial learning, and multi-kernel maximum mean discrepancy at the temporal and trial levels. At inference time, an information-maximization stage adapts only normalization parameters using unlabeled evaluation-session trials and re-estimates the AutoDIAL target statistics; their labels remain hidden until scoring. Across nine LOSO target subjects, Ours achieved $75.81\pm10.63\%$ session-2 accuracy on BCI IV-2a and $83.41\pm10.35\%$ sessions~4--5 accuracy on BCI IV-2b, with Cohen's $\kappa$ scores of $67.70\pm14.20$ and $66.80\pm20.70$, respectively, on the $\times100$ scale. Across the four Zhou2016 targets, it reached $74.00\pm6.02\%$ session-3 accuracy ($\kappa=61.00\pm9.03$). Code will be released at \url{https://github.com/USERNAME/REPOSITORY}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Motor-imagery brain--computer interfaces (MI-BCIs) decode movement intentions from non-invasive electroencephalography (EEG), with applications in rehabilitation, assistive communication, and human--machine control. Their principal signatures, event-related desynchronization and synchronization, are weak, non-stationary, and distributed across time and electrodes \cite{pfurtscheller1999erd}. They also vary substantially among subjects and recording sessions. Consequently, a model trained on existing users often degrades on an unseen user, whereas acquiring labeled calibration trials for every user is time-consuming. Cross-subject decoding must therefore learn discriminative spatial--temporal structure from limited EEG while controlling inter-subject distribution shift without target labels.

Classical MI pipelines rely on common spatial pattern (CSP), which maximizes class-specific variance, and filter-bank CSP (FBCSP), which extends this idea across frequency bands \cite{ramoser2000optimal,ang2012filterbank}. Although effective and interpretable, these methods separate filtering, feature extraction, and classification into manually designed stages; conventional CSP is also naturally binary and sensitive to the available trials and frequency range. Their subject-specific covariance structure can therefore transfer poorly when the user changes.

End-to-end convolutional neural networks reduce this dependence on handcrafted processing. Deep and shallow ConvNets learn temporal and electrode filters directly from EEG, while EEGNet uses depthwise and separable convolutions to obtain compact frequency-specific spatial features \cite{schirrmeister2017deep,lawhern2018eegnet}. Multi-scale temporal kernels are especially relevant because MI information is not confined to one frequency or duration. However, convolutions connect distant time points only through additional depth, larger kernels, or dilation, which may be insufficient to characterize the full temporal evolution of a trial.

Hybrid decoders therefore combine EEG-specific convolution with global sequence modeling. EEG Conformer applies self-attention after convolutional tokenization, ATCNet combines attention with a dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN), and TCFormer integrates a multi-kernel front-end, grouped-query Transformer layers, and a grouped TCN head \cite{song2023eegconformer,altaheri2023atcnet,altaheri2025tcformer}. These models demonstrate the value of combining local spectral--spatial priors with longer temporal context. Nevertheless, self-attention explicitly constructs pairwise token interactions and has quadratic sequence complexity \cite{vaswani2017attention}. This cost becomes undesirable for long, high-resolution EEG, while highly flexible attention operators may also be data-hungry in the small-sample regimes common in BCI studies.

Architecture alone cannot remove subject shift. Target-free domain generalization learns invariant representations from source subjects \cite{liu2023stdg,zhong2025eegdg,zheng2025crosssubject}, whereas unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) may additionally exploit unlabeled target EEG. The distinction is important: observing target signals without labels is UDA rather than zero-shot generalization. Existing UDA methods reduce covariance mismatch through Riemannian or Euclidean alignment \cite{zanini2018riemannian,he2020euclidean}, minimize feature discrepancy with MK-MMD \cite{long2015dan}, or promote domain confusion through gradient reversal \cite{ganin2016dann,tang2020cdan}. HADANet combines adversarial and MK-MMD alignment for MI-EEG \cite{wu2026hadanet}, while prototype-based methods additionally exploit local class structure \cite{zhang2024prototypeda}. Domain shift can also be reduced inside normalization layers: AutoDIAL standardizes target features with a learned mixture of source and target statistics, so the network itself determines how much domain-specific normalization is beneficial \cite{carlucci2017autodial}. These approaches remain vulnerable to negative transfer: marginal alignment may mix classes, and aligning only pooled trial features can overlook temporal mismatch.

Residual shift may also be addressed at inference. Information-maximization methods encourage confident but globally diverse predictions without labels \cite{liang2020shot}, and TENT restricts adaptation to normalization parameters through entropy minimization \cite{wang2021tent}. Such constraints are attractive for EEG because target datasets are small and unrestricted fine-tuning can overfit or collapse to one predicted class. However, evaluation-session access must be disclosed explicitly, and labels must remain unavailable during adaptation and checkpoint selection.

Selective state-space models offer a linear-complexity alternative to attention. S4 introduced structured long-range sequence modeling \cite{gu2022s4}, and Mamba made transition quantities input-dependent so that relevant information can be retained selectively \cite{gu2024mamba}. Recent MI decoders have combined convolution and Mamba \cite{guo2025mimamba,yang2025stmamba}, but primarily study architecture rather than leakage-controlled integration with target adaptation. Moreover, a generic state-space stack should complement, rather than replace, explicit temporal and electrode filtering. A purely forward scan also imposes unnecessary directionality when complete offline MI trials are available.

We therefore present an improved cross-subject MI-EEG decoding framework that jointly addresses temporal representation and subject shift. The framework learns complementary local and long-range EEG dynamics, reduces distribution mismatch during training, and performs constrained label-free adaptation at inference. Target labels remain hidden throughout preprocessing, training, checkpoint selection, and adaptation.

The contributions of this study are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item an improved end-to-end framework for cross-subject MI-EEG decoding that unifies multi-scale local representation, selective long-range temporal modeling, and local--global feature fusion;
    \item a hierarchical adaptation strategy with a chronological, leakage-controlled LOSO protocol that separates source learning, unlabeled target adaptation, and held-out evaluation; and
    \item a comprehensive evaluation on three public MI-EEG datasets using subject-wise accuracy, Cohen's $\kappa$, efficiency analysis, and component ablations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{BCI Competition IV-2a}
\label{subsec:bcic2a}

BCI Competition IV dataset 2a contains recordings from nine participants. Each participant completed two sessions on different days, and each session contains 288 trials distributed equally over imagined left-hand, right-hand, both-feet, and tongue movements. Signals were acquired from 22 EEG electrodes and three EOG electrodes at 250~Hz; only the EEG electrodes are supplied to the classifier. The original recordings were hardware band-pass filtered between 0.5 and 100~Hz and notch filtered at 50~Hz \cite{tangermann2012review}. The implementation uses the event-defined MI interval exposed by MOABB, rescales volts to microvolts, and does not apply an additional frequency filter. The first recording session is denoted session~1 and the second is denoted session~2 throughout this paper.

\subsection{BCI Competition IV-2b}
\label{subsec:bcic2b}

BCI Competition IV dataset 2b contains two-class left- versus right-hand MI recordings from nine participants. Three bipolar EEG derivations centered on C3, Cz, and C4 were sampled at 250~Hz, together with three EOG channels that are excluded from decoding. Each participant completed five sessions: the first three form the calibration portion and the final two form the original evaluation portion \cite{tangermann2012review}. The present loader follows this natural chronological separation. The event-defined MI window is used after removing the final 0.5~s through the configured stop offset. This dataset tests whether the method remains useful when spatial coverage is reduced from 22 electrodes to only three.

\subsection{Zhou2016}
\label{subsec:zhou2016}

The Zhou2016 dataset contains four participants, each recorded in three sessions separated by days to months. A session consists of two runs of 75 trials, with 25 trials per class, giving 450 trials per participant. The three MI classes are left hand, right hand, and feet. EEG was recorded from 14 electrodes at 250~Hz. Each trial contains a 5-s visual arrow period during which the indicated imagery is performed, followed by a 4-s rest period \cite{zhou2016trialselection}. The public data are loaded through the MOABB representation \cite{jayaram2018moabb}; the dataset-specific frequency and time-window processing is described in Section~\ref{subsec:preprocessing}.

\section{Proposed Method}
\label{sec:method}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture_overview} summarizes one LOSO fold. A shared encoder processes labeled source trials and unlabeled target trials during Stage~1 UDA; an AutoDIAL layer then normalizes the pooled trial features before the residual aligner, classifier, and alignment losses. After source-validation checkpoint selection, Stage~2 adapts only normalization parameters with unlabeled evaluation-session EEG and re-estimates the AutoDIAL target statistics; labels are revealed only for Stage~3 scoring.

\subsection{Problem formulation}

Let $\mathcal{S}=\{(X_i^s,y_i^s)\}_{i=1}^{N_s}$ be labeled trials from the source subjects, $\mathcal{T}_a=\{X_j^a\}_{j=1}^{N_a}$ the earlier unlabeled target sessions used for UDA, and $\mathcal{T}_e=\{X_j^e\}_{j=1}^{N_e}$ the later target sessions used for evaluation, where $X\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times T}$. Training uses $\mathcal{S}$ and $\mathcal{T}_a$; IM-TTA may access only the signal values of $\mathcal{T}_e$, never their labels.

\subsection{Leakage-safe signal alignment}
\label{subsec:ra}

A separate Riemannian whitening operator is optionally fitted for each subject. OAS trial covariances are summarized by their affine-invariant mean $\bar{R}$, and trials are transformed as
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{X}=\bar{R}^{-1/2}X.
\end{equation}
A source transform is fitted on that subject's training sessions and applied to its validation sessions; the target transform is fitted on $\mathcal{T}_a$ and frozen before application to $\mathcal{T}_e$ \cite{zanini2018riemannian,he2020euclidean}. Evaluation-session data therefore update neither the Riemannian transforms nor the input z-score statistics; the only statistics estimated on $\mathcal{T}_e$ are the AutoDIAL target moments in Stage~2 (Section~\ref{subsec:imtta}).

\subsection{Multi-scale convolutional encoder}
\label{subsec:conv_frontend}

As detailed in Fig.~\ref{fig:encoder_detail}, three temporal convolutions with kernels of 20, 32, and 64 samples produce 32 maps each \cite{altaheri2025tcformer}. Their concatenated output is processed by depthwise convolution across all electrodes with depth multiplier two, average pooling, and dropout. A grouped pointwise projection reduces the 192 spatial maps to 48 channels (16 per temporal scale), followed by grouped temporal convolution and residual channel-group attention. The latter assigns one learned coefficient to each scale. A second pooling layer yields $H_c\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times48\times L}$.

(b) Channel-group attention: grouped $1\times1$ squeeze--excitation yields one sigmoid weight per temporal scale, which rescales that scale's 16 channels and is added residually.}

\subsection{Alternating selective state-space encoder}
\label{subsec:ssm}

After pointwise channel mixing, five selective state-space blocks scan in the order forward--backward--forward--backward--forward. A backward block reverses the temporal axis before and after its recurrence, providing bidirectional offline context without duplicating every block. Within each block, layer normalization, gated projections, and a causal depthwise convolution generate $u_t$. A diagonal selective recurrence then computes \cite{gu2022s4,gu2024mamba}
\begin{align}
    A &= -\exp(A_{\log}), \\
    h_t &= \exp(\Delta_t A)\odot h_{t-1}
       + \Delta_t B_t\odot u_t, \\
    z_t &= C_t^\top h_t + d_{\mathrm{skip}}\odot u_t .
\end{align}
Here $\Delta_t$, $B_t$, and $C_t$ depend on the input. The implementation uses state dimension eight, dropout 0.4, and depth-dependent stochastic depth. It is a lightweight PyTorch selective SSM rather than the official hardware-aware Mamba kernel, but retains linear scaling in sequence length.

\subsection{Local--global fusion and classification}
\label{subsec:tcn}

The state-space output is reduced from 48 to 16 channels and concatenated with the 48-channel shortcut $H_c$. Two residual grouped TCN blocks with kernel length four and dilations one and two refine the resulting 64-channel sequence. The mean of its last state and temporal average forms the trial representation. A norm-constrained grouped $1\times1$ classifier produces four group-wise logits, which are combined by learned softmax weights.

\subsection{Automatic domain alignment layer}
\label{subsec:autodial}

Let $q\in\mathbb{R}^{64}$ denote a pooled trial representation, formed by the 16-channel groups of the three convolutional scales and the state-space branch. Following AutoDIAL \cite{carlucci2017autodial}, an alignment layer standardizes source features with source statistics $(\mu_s,\sigma_s^2)$, whereas target features are standardized with the moments of the source--target mixture weighted by $\alpha$:
\begin{align}
    \mu_\alpha &= \alpha\mu_t+(1-\alpha)\mu_s, \\
    \sigma_\alpha^2 &= \alpha\sigma_t^2+(1-\alpha)\sigma_s^2+\alpha(1-\alpha)(\mu_t-\mu_s)^2, \\
    \hat{q}^s &= \gamma\odot\frac{q^s-\mu_s}{\sqrt{\sigma_s^2+\epsilon}}+\beta, \qquad
    \hat{q}^t = \gamma\odot\frac{q^t-\mu_\alpha}{\sqrt{\sigma_\alpha^2+\epsilon}}+\beta .
\end{align}
The affine parameters $\gamma$ and $\beta$ are shared by both domains. A separate $\alpha=\mathrm{sigmoid}(a)\in(0,1)$, initialized to 0.5, is learned for each of the four feature groups: $\alpha\rightarrow0$ normalizes the target with source statistics, whereas $\alpha\rightarrow1$ recovers fully domain-specific normalization. During training, mini-batch moments of each domain are used and running estimates are updated with momentum 0.1; at inference, the running source and target moments are mixed with the learned $\alpha$. Source validation trials are always normalized with source statistics.

\subsection{Hierarchical unsupervised domain adaptation}
\label{subsec:uda}

A residual $64\!\rightarrow\!128\!\rightarrow\!64$ feature aligner follows the AutoDIAL layer and precedes classification and domain alignment. Source cross-entropy $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}$ maintains discrimination. A gradient-reversal discriminator provides domain loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{adv}}$ \cite{ganin2016dann}, while five-kernel MK-MMD aligns normalized source and target representations \cite{long2015dan}. It is applied both to pooled trial features ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mmd}}$) and to time-averaged TCN sequences ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tmmd}}$). In addition, the information-maximization objective $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IM}}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:im} is evaluated on the predictions for the unlabeled target mini-batch ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IM}}^{t}$), giving
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}+0.1\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IM}}^{t}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{adv}}
    +0.5\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mmd}}+0.1\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tmmd}}.
\label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
The alignment losses $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{adv}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mmd}}$, and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tmmd}}$ see the AutoDIAL output with $\alpha$ held fixed, because they could otherwise be reduced trivially by driving $\alpha$ toward one. Since source features do not depend on $\alpha$, the mixing weights are learned only through $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IM}}^{t}$, that is, toward the normalization that yields confident yet class-diverse target predictions. Only $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}$ uses labels, all of which come from source subjects; target trials in this stage come exclusively from $\mathcal{T}_a$. The discriminator coefficient increases smoothly from zero to one with training progress.

\subsection{Constrained information-maximization adaptation}
\label{subsec:imtta}

After checkpoint selection, all parameters except BatchNorm affine weights and biases are frozen. Five Adam passes over unlabeled $\mathcal{T}_e$ at learning rate $10^{-4}$ minimize
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IM}}
    =\frac{1}{N_e}\sum_j H(p_j)-H\!\left(\frac{1}{N_e}\sum_jp_j\right),
\label{eq:im}
\end{equation}
where the first term encourages confident predictions and the second preserves class diversity \cite{liang2020shot,wang2021tent}. Because IM-TTA changes the backbone features, the AutoDIAL target moments $(\mu_t,\sigma_t^2)$ are then re-estimated in one pass over the same unlabeled $\mathcal{T}_e$ trials, while the learned $\alpha$ and the source moments remain fixed. No evaluation label or metric is used for adaptation, re-estimation, or checkpoint selection.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Chronological LOSO protocol with test-time adaptation}
\label{subsec:clean_protocol}

\subsection{Preprocessing and augmentation}
\label{subsec:preprocessing}

Only EEG channels are retained. Signals are resampled to 250~Hz and converted from volts to microvolts. The BCI Competition datasets receive no additional digital band-pass filtering: BCI IV-2a uses the complete MOABB event window, while BCI IV-2b removes 0.5~s from its end. For Zhou2016, all 14 EEG channels are band-pass filtered at 8--30~Hz and the interval from 0.5 to 5.0~s after cue onset is retained (4.5~s, or 1125 samples). This setting suppresses the initial cue transient and focuses both feature extraction and covariance estimation on the motor-imagery $\mu/\beta$ range.

Riemannian whitening, when active, follows the session-isolated fitting rule in Section~\ref{subsec:ra}. Z-score statistics are fitted using the source-training partition and then frozen. Inter-trial segment reconstruction augmentation is applied only to labeled source mini-batches: segments are recombined among trials of the same source class, so it neither reads target labels nor creates mixed source--target examples. Zhou2016 is run without this augmentation to preserve its intended dataset-specific setting.

\subsection{Training configuration}
\label{subsec:training}

All experiments are executed in Kaggle notebooks using one NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU with 16~GB memory. BCI IV-2a and BCI IV-2b use a mini-batch size of 48 and a maximum of 125 epochs. Zhou2016 uses a mini-batch size of 32 and 100 epochs. The BCI Competition runs use Adam with an initial learning rate of $9\times10^{-4}$, $\beta_1=0.5$, and weight decay $10^{-3}$; the Zhou2016 setting uses an initial learning rate of $10^{-3}$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$. A linear warm-up over the first three LOSO epochs is followed by cosine decay. The random seed, data split, and initialization procedure are held fixed for direct architectural ablations. Final reporting should additionally repeat each complete LOSO experiment with multiple seeds to quantify optimization variance.

\subsection{Evaluation measures and reporting}
\label{subsec:metrics}

Accuracy and Cohen's $\kappa$ ($\times100$) are reported per target subject and as mean $\pm$ standard deviation across LOSO folds. Paired accuracy differences use a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test with exact sign-permutation $p$-values. Efficiency is summarized by trainable parameters, mean Stage~1 training time per fold on a Tesla T4, and batch-one CPU response time averaged over 500 passes after 100 warm-up passes; IM-TTA, preprocessing, and data transfer are excluded from these timings.

\subsection{Baseline comparison policy}
\label{subsec:baselines}

All methods follow the same LOSO folds, chronological split, and target-data access in Table~\ref{tab:protocol}; results obtained under incompatible protocols are not copied. EEG Conformer \cite{song2023eegconformer}, ATCNet \cite{altaheri2023atcnet}, TCFormer \cite{altaheri2025tcformer}, DAN \cite{long2015dan}, and CDAN \cite{tang2020cdan} are the selected baselines. The proposed method is reported in three variants:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ours-S}: source-only training with $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}$;
    \item \textbf{Ours-U}: Stage~1 UDA, including AutoDIAL, without IM-TTA; the AutoDIAL target statistics are still re-estimated on unlabeled $\mathcal{T}_e$; and
    \item \textbf{Ours}: Ours-U followed by label-free IM-TTA on the evaluation-session EEG.
\end{itemize}
Thus, Ours-U--Ours-S isolates Stage~1 adaptation, while Ours--Ours-U isolates IM-TTA.

The Zhou2016 Ours-S, Ours-U, and w/o-RA runs therefore use a loop-hoisted scan: $\exp(\Delta_t A)$, $\Delta_t B_t\odot u_t$, and the $C_t$ readout are computed for all time steps outside the recurrence, leaving one multiply--add per step, and the causal depthwise convolution is evaluated as shifted multiply--adds. \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results show that adaptation is dataset dependent. The complete framework produced a clear gain on BCI IV-2a, whereas its improvement on BCI IV-2b was modest. On Zhou2016, the variants without RA or IM-TTA outperformed the complete configuration, while source-only and no-DA training also remained competitive. This outcome may reflect the small four-subject sample, long inter-session gaps, and subject-specific spectral variation. Although filtering to 8--30~Hz and excluding the first 0.5~s focus covariance estimation on MI-related activity rather than broadband drift and cue transients, repeated-seed experiments are still required to establish the stability of the observed RA and IM-TTA sensitivity.

Architecturally, multi-scale and depthwise convolutions preserve local spectral--spatial evidence, while alternating selective scans and the TCN model complementary long- and short-range dynamics. The alternating scans exploit complete offline trials and are non-causal; online use would require forward-only processing and device-specific latency validation.

The method is transductive because IM-TTA accesses unlabeled evaluation-session signals before scoring. Comparisons must therefore provide baselines with identical target access. Other limitations include the three-channel BCI IV-2b setting, the small Zhou2016 cohort, and the absence of repeated-seed and fully matched baseline results.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a cross-subject MI-EEG framework combining multi-scale spatial--temporal convolution, alternating selective state-space blocks, local--global TCN fusion, hierarchical UDA, and constrained IM-TTA. Its chronological LOSO protocol excludes target labels from preprocessing, training, adaptation, and checkpoint selection. Ours obtained $75.81\pm10.63\%$ session-2 accuracy on BCI IV-2a and $83.41\pm10.35\%$ sessions~4--5 accuracy on BCI IV-2b, with corresponding Cohen's $\kappa$ scores of $67.70\pm14.20$ and $66.80\pm20.70$ on the $\times100$ scale. On Zhou2016, Ours obtained $74.00\pm6.02\%$ target-session-3 accuracy and $61.00\pm9.03$ Cohen's $\kappa$. Matched baselines and repeated-seed experiments remain necessary before drawing broader conclusions.
