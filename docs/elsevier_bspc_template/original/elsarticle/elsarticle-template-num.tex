\begin{abstract}
Cross-subject motor-imagery electroencephalography (MI-EEG) decoding is limited by low signal-to-noise ratios, scarce labeled data, and strong distribution shifts across subjects and sessions. We propose a domain-adaptive selective state-space framework for this setting. A multi-kernel temporal convolutional front-end with depthwise spatial filtering and channel-group attention extracts local spectral--spatial features. Five selective state-space blocks with alternating forward and backward scans then model long-range temporal context at a cost linear in sequence length, and a convolutional shortcut with a grouped dilated temporal convolutional network fuses local and global information. To reduce cross-subject shift, the framework combines per-subject Riemannian alignment, an automatic domain alignment (AutoDIAL) layer that learns how strongly target features are normalized with target rather than source statistics, gradient-reversal adversarial learning, and multi-kernel maximum mean discrepancy at the temporal and trial levels. At inference time, an information-maximization stage adapts only normalization parameters using unlabeled evaluation-session trials and re-estimates the AutoDIAL target statistics; their labels remain hidden until scoring. Across nine LOSO target subjects, Ours achieved $75.81\pm10.63\%$ session-2 accuracy on BCI IV-2a and $83.41\pm10.35\%$ sessions~4--5 accuracy on BCI IV-2b, with Cohen's $\kappa$ scores of $67.70\pm14.20$ and $66.80\pm20.70$, respectively, on the $\times100$ scale. Across the four Zhou2016 targets, it reached $84.33\pm0.75\%$ session-3 accuracy ($\kappa=76.50\pm1.12$). Code will be released at \url{https://github.com/USERNAME/REPOSITORY}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Motor-imagery brain--computer interfaces (MI-BCIs) decode movement intentions from non-invasive electroencephalography (EEG) for rehabilitation, assistive communication, and human--machine control. Their principal signatures, event-related desynchronization and synchronization, are weak, non-stationary, and distributed across time and electrodes \cite{pfurtscheller1999erd}, and they vary substantially between subjects and recording sessions. A model trained on existing users therefore often degrades on a new user, while collecting labeled calibration trials for every user is time-consuming. Cross-subject decoding must learn discriminative spatial--temporal structure from limited EEG and control inter-subject shift without target labels.

Existing work addresses this problem only partly. Convolutional and attention-based decoders learn strong spatial--temporal features but are trained on source subjects alone, whereas domain-adaptation methods reduce subject shift but are usually attached to generic backbones and evaluated under protocols in which the use of target-session data is not always explicit (Section~\ref{sec:related}).

We therefore propose a cross-subject MI-EEG framework that couples an EEG-specific selective state-space encoder with hierarchical, label-free adaptation, and evaluate it under a chronological leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) protocol in which target labels remain hidden throughout preprocessing, training, checkpoint selection, and adaptation. The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item an end-to-end decoder that combines multi-scale convolution, alternating forward/backward selective state-space blocks, and local--global TCN fusion;
    \item a hierarchical adaptation strategy that combines Riemannian alignment, an AutoDIAL normalization layer, adversarial and MK-MMD alignment, and constrained test-time adaptation within a chronological, leakage-controlled LOSO protocol; and
    \item an evaluation on three public MI-EEG datasets with subject-wise accuracy, Cohen's $\kappa$, statistical tests, efficiency analysis, and component ablations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Convolutional and attention-based MI decoders}
\label{subsec:rw_decoders}

Classical pipelines rely on common spatial pattern (CSP) and filter-bank CSP (FBCSP), which optimize class-specific variance in manually designed stages \cite{ramoser2000optimal,ang2012filterbank}; their subject-specific covariance structure transfers poorly across users. Deep and shallow ConvNets and EEGNet learn temporal and electrode filters end to end \cite{schirrmeister2017deep,lawhern2018eegnet}, but convolutions connect distant time points only through depth, large kernels, or dilation. Hybrid decoders therefore add global sequence modeling: EEG Conformer applies self-attention after convolutional tokenization, ATCNet combines attention with a dilated temporal convolutional network (TCN), and TCFormer integrates a multi-kernel front-end, grouped-query Transformer layers, and a grouped TCN head \cite{song2023eegconformer,altaheri2023atcnet,altaheri2025tcformer}. Self-attention, however, has quadratic sequence complexity \cite{vaswani2017attention} and can be data-hungry in the small-sample regimes typical of BCI studies.

\subsection{Selective state-space models}
\label{subsec:rw_ssm}

State-space models offer linear-complexity sequence modeling. S4 introduced structured long-range state spaces \cite{gu2022s4}, and Mamba made the transition quantities input dependent so that relevant information is retained selectively \cite{gu2024mamba}. Recent MI decoders combine convolution with Mamba \cite{guo2025mimamba,yang2025stmamba}, but they primarily study the architecture rather than its leakage-controlled integration with target adaptation, and a purely forward scan imposes unnecessary directionality when complete offline trials are available.

\subsection{Cross-subject domain adaptation}
\label{subsec:rw_da}

Domain generalization learns subject-invariant representations from source subjects only \cite{liu2023stdg,zhong2025eegdg,zheng2025crosssubject}, whereas unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) also exploits unlabeled target EEG. UDA methods reduce covariance mismatch through Riemannian or Euclidean alignment \cite{zanini2018riemannian,he2020euclidean}, minimize feature discrepancy with MK-MMD \cite{long2015dan}, or promote domain confusion through gradient reversal \cite{ganin2016dann,tang2020cdan}. HADANet combines adversarial and MK-MMD alignment for MI-EEG \cite{wu2026hadanet}, and prototype-based methods additionally exploit class structure \cite{zhang2024prototypeda}. Alignment can also act inside normalization layers: AutoDIAL standardizes target features with a learned mixture of source and target statistics \cite{carlucci2017autodial}. Marginal alignment nevertheless remains vulnerable to negative transfer, because it may mix classes and, when applied only to pooled trial features, can overlook temporal mismatch.

\subsection{Test-time adaptation}
\label{subsec:rw_tta}

Residual shift can also be reduced at inference. Information maximization encourages confident but class-diverse predictions without labels \cite{liang2020shot}, and Tent restricts adaptation to normalization parameters \cite{wang2021tent}. Such constraints suit small EEG target sets, where unrestricted fine-tuning can overfit or collapse to one class, but they access evaluation-session signals and therefore require explicit disclosure and strict separation from labels.

\subsection{Research gap}
\label{subsec:gap}

Three gaps follow. First, selective state-space MI decoders have not been combined with target adaptation, and their forward-only scans ignore the offline availability of complete trials. Second, EEG adaptation methods align features with a fixed strength and mostly at the pooled-trial level; they neither learn how far target normalization should depart from source statistics nor align temporal representations. Third, the unlabeled target data used for adaptation are not always separated from, or disclosed relative to, the evaluation data. The proposed framework addresses these gaps with a bidirectional selective state-space encoder (Section~\ref{subsec:ssm}), a hierarchical adaptation scheme that includes AutoDIAL normalization and temporal MK-MMD (Sections~\ref{subsec:autodial}--\ref{subsec:imtta}), and a chronological LOSO protocol that discloses every use of unlabeled target data (Section~\ref{subsec:clean_protocol}).

\section{Proposed Method}
\label{sec:method}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture_overview} summarizes one LOSO fold. A shared encoder processes labeled source trials and unlabeled target trials during Stage~1 UDA; an AutoDIAL layer then normalizes the pooled trial features before the residual aligner, classifier, and alignment losses. After source-validation checkpoint selection, Stage~2 adapts only normalization parameters with unlabeled evaluation-session EEG and re-estimates the AutoDIAL target statistics; labels are revealed only for Stage~3 scoring.

\subsection{Problem formulation}

Let $\mathcal{S}=\{(X_i^s,y_i^s)\}_{i=1}^{N_s}$ be labeled trials from the source subjects, $\mathcal{T}_a=\{X_j^a\}_{j=1}^{N_a}$ the earlier unlabeled target sessions used for UDA, and $\mathcal{T}_e=\{X_j^e\}_{j=1}^{N_e}$ the later target sessions used for evaluation, where $X\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times T}$. Training uses $\mathcal{S}$ and $\mathcal{T}_a$; IM-TTA may access only the signal values of $\mathcal{T}_e$, never their labels.

\subsection{Leakage-safe signal alignment}
\label{subsec:ra}

A separate Riemannian whitening operator is optionally fitted for each subject. OAS trial covariances are summarized by their affine-invariant mean $\bar{R}$, and trials are transformed as
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{X}=\bar{R}^{-1/2}X.
\end{equation}
A source transform is fitted on that subject's training sessions and applied to its validation sessions; the target transform is fitted on $\mathcal{T}_a$ and frozen before application to $\mathcal{T}_e$ \cite{zanini2018riemannian,he2020euclidean}. Evaluation-session data therefore update neither the Riemannian transforms nor the input z-score statistics; the only statistics estimated on $\mathcal{T}_e$ are the AutoDIAL target moments in Stage~2 (Section~\ref{subsec:imtta}).

\subsection{Multi-scale convolutional encoder}
\label{subsec:conv_frontend}

As detailed in Fig.~\ref{fig:encoder_detail}, three temporal convolutions with kernels of 20, 32, and 64 samples produce 32 maps each \cite{altaheri2025tcformer}. Their concatenated output is processed by depthwise convolution across all electrodes with depth multiplier two, average pooling, and dropout. A grouped pointwise projection reduces the 192 spatial maps to 48 channels (16 per temporal scale), followed by grouped temporal convolution and residual channel-group attention. The latter assigns one learned coefficient to each scale. A second pooling layer yields $H_c\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times48\times L}$.

(b) Channel-group attention: grouped $1\times1$ squeeze--excitation yields one sigmoid weight per temporal scale, which rescales that scale's 16 channels and is added residually.}

\subsection{Alternating selective state-space encoder}
\label{subsec:ssm}

After pointwise channel mixing, five selective state-space blocks scan in the order forward--backward--forward--backward--forward. A backward block reverses the temporal axis before and after its recurrence, providing bidirectional offline context without duplicating every block (Fig.~\ref{fig:ssm_block}). Within each block, layer normalization, gated projections, and a causal depthwise convolution generate $u_t$. A diagonal selective recurrence then computes \cite{gu2022s4,gu2024mamba}
\begin{align}
    A &= -\exp(A_{\log}), \\
    h_t &= \exp(\Delta_t A)\odot h_{t-1}
       + \Delta_t B_t\odot u_t, \\
    z_t &= C_t^\top h_t + d_{\mathrm{skip}}\odot u_t .
\end{align}
Here $\Delta_t$, $B_t$, and $C_t$ depend on the input. The implementation uses state dimension eight, dropout 0.4, and depth-dependent stochastic depth. It is a lightweight PyTorch selective SSM rather than the official hardware-aware Mamba kernel, but retains linear scaling in sequence length.

    \caption{Selective SSM block. Backward blocks reverse and restore the temporal axis around the mixer; forward blocks omit these operations.}

\end{figure*}

\subsection{Local--global fusion and classification}
\label{subsec:tcn}

The state-space output is reduced from 48 to 16 channels and concatenated with the 48-channel shortcut $H_c$. Two residual grouped TCN blocks with kernel length four and dilations one and two refine the resulting 64-channel sequence. The mean of its last state and temporal average forms the trial representation. A norm-constrained grouped $1\times1$ classifier produces four group-wise logits, which are combined by learned softmax weights.

\subsection{Automatic domain alignment layer}
\label{subsec:autodial}

Let $q\in\mathbb{R}^{64}$ denote a pooled trial representation, formed by the 16-channel groups of the three convolutional scales and the state-space branch. Following AutoDIAL \cite{carlucci2017autodial}, an alignment layer standardizes source features with source statistics $(\mu_s,\sigma_s^2)$, whereas target features are standardized with the moments of the source--target mixture weighted by $\alpha$:
\begin{align}
    \mu_\alpha &= \alpha\mu_t+(1-\alpha)\mu_s, \\
    \sigma_\alpha^2 &= \alpha\sigma_t^2+(1-\alpha)\sigma_s^2+\alpha(1-\alpha)(\mu_t-\mu_s)^2, \\
    \hat{q}^s &= \gamma\odot\frac{q^s-\mu_s}{\sqrt{\sigma_s^2+\epsilon}}+\beta, \qquad
    \hat{q}^t = \gamma\odot\frac{q^t-\mu_\alpha}{\sqrt{\sigma_\alpha^2+\epsilon}}+\beta .
\end{align}
The affine parameters $\gamma$ and $\beta$ are shared by both domains. A separate $\alpha=\mathrm{sigmoid}(a)\in(0,1)$, initialized to 0.5, is learned for each of the four feature groups: $\alpha\rightarrow0$ normalizes the target with source statistics, whereas $\alpha\rightarrow1$ recovers fully domain-specific normalization. During training, mini-batch moments of each domain are used and running estimates are updated with momentum 0.1; at inference, the running source and target moments are mixed with the learned $\alpha$. Source validation trials are always normalized with source statistics.

\subsection{Hierarchical unsupervised domain adaptation}
\label{subsec:uda}

A residual $64\!\rightarrow\!128\!\rightarrow\!64$ feature aligner follows the AutoDIAL layer and precedes classification and domain alignment. Source cross-entropy $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}$ maintains discrimination. A gradient-reversal discriminator provides domain loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{adv}}$ \cite{ganin2016dann}, while five-kernel MK-MMD aligns normalized source and target representations \cite{long2015dan}. It is applied both to pooled trial features ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mmd}}$) and to time-averaged TCN sequences ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tmmd}}$). In addition, the information-maximization objective $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IM}}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:im} is evaluated on the predictions for the unlabeled target mini-batch ($\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IM}}^{t}$), giving
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}+0.1\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IM}}^{t}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{adv}}
    +0.5\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mmd}}+0.1\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tmmd}}.
\label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
The alignment losses $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{adv}}$, $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{mmd}}$, and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tmmd}}$ see the AutoDIAL output with $\alpha$ held fixed, because they could otherwise be reduced trivially by driving $\alpha$ toward one. Since source features do not depend on $\alpha$, the mixing weights are learned only through $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IM}}^{t}$, that is, toward the normalization that yields confident yet class-diverse target predictions. Only $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}$ uses labels, all of which come from source subjects; target trials in this stage come exclusively from $\mathcal{T}_a$. The discriminator coefficient increases smoothly from zero to one with training progress.

\subsection{Constrained information-maximization adaptation}
\label{subsec:imtta}

After checkpoint selection, all parameters except BatchNorm affine weights and biases are frozen. Five Adam passes over unlabeled $\mathcal{T}_e$ at learning rate $10^{-4}$ minimize
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{IM}}
    =\frac{1}{N_e}\sum_j H(p_j)-H\!\left(\frac{1}{N_e}\sum_jp_j\right),
\label{eq:im}
\end{equation}
where the first term encourages confident predictions and the second preserves class diversity \cite{liang2020shot,wang2021tent}. Because IM-TTA changes the backbone features, the AutoDIAL target moments $(\mu_t,\sigma_t^2)$ are then re-estimated in one pass over the same unlabeled $\mathcal{T}_e$ trials, while the learned $\alpha$ and the source moments remain fixed. No evaluation label or metric is used for adaptation, re-estimation, or checkpoint selection.

\subsubsection{BCI Competition IV-2a}
\label{subsec:bcic2a}

The dataset contains nine participants recorded in two sessions on different days; each session has 288 trials distributed equally over imagined left-hand, right-hand, both-feet, and tongue movements. EEG was recorded from 22 electrodes (three additional EOG channels are not used) at 250~Hz with hardware band-pass filtering at 0.5--100~Hz and a 50-Hz notch \cite{tangermann2012review}. The two sessions are denoted session~1 and session~2. Participant sex and age are not reported.

\subsubsection{BCI Competition IV-2b}
\label{subsec:bcic2b}

The dataset contains two-class left- versus right-hand MI from nine right-handed participants with normal or corrected-to-normal vision, drawn from a study of ten participants (six male, four female; age $24.7\pm3.3$ years) \cite{leeb2007virtual}; the sex of the nine released participants is not reported individually. Three bipolar derivations around C3, Cz, and C4 (three EOG channels are not used) were sampled at 250~Hz. Sessions 1--3 form the original calibration portion and sessions 4--5 the evaluation portion \cite{tangermann2012review}, a chronological separation that is kept here. The dataset tests whether the method remains useful with only three electrodes.

\subsubsection{Zhou2016}
\label{subsec:zhou2016}

The dataset contains four healthy participants (one male and three female, aged 22--28 years; sex reported only at group level), each recorded in three sessions separated by days to months \cite{zhou2016trialselection}. Each session has two runs of 75 trials (25 per class: left hand, right hand, and feet), recorded from 14 electrodes at 250~Hz. Each trial contains a 5-s cue period, during which the imagery is performed, followed by a 4-s rest.

\subsection{Chronological LOSO protocol with test-time adaptation}
\label{subsec:clean_protocol}

One subject is held out in each LOSO fold. All remaining subjects are sources; the held-out participant is the target. Table~\ref{tab:protocol} specifies the chronological session split. Earlier target sessions are used without labels during Stage~1 UDA. The later evaluation sessions remain unavailable during model training, preprocessing-statistic fitting, and checkpoint selection; at inference time, their EEG signals are passed without labels to IM-TTA and AutoDIAL target-statistic re-estimation, after which the adapted model is scored using the withheld labels. This is the primary protocol for headline comparisons.

For Zhou2016, an all-session target score is additionally reported as an auxiliary diagnostic only.


\subsection{Preprocessing and augmentation}
\label{subsec:preprocessing}

Only EEG channels are retained. Signals are resampled to 250~Hz and converted from volts to microvolts. The BCI Competition datasets receive no additional digital band-pass filtering: BCI IV-2a uses the complete MOABB event window, while BCI IV-2b removes 0.5~s from its end. For Zhou2016, all 14 EEG channels are band-pass filtered at 8--30~Hz and the interval from 0.5 to 5.0~s after cue onset is retained (4.5~s, or 1125 samples). This setting suppresses the initial cue transient and focuses both feature extraction and covariance estimation on the motor-imagery $\mu/\beta$ range.

Riemannian whitening, when active, follows the session-isolated fitting rule in Section~\ref{subsec:ra}. Z-score statistics are fitted using the source-training partition and then frozen. Inter-trial segment reconstruction augmentation is applied only to labeled source mini-batches: segments are recombined among trials of the same source class, so it neither reads target labels nor creates mixed source--target examples. Zhou2016 is run without this augmentation to preserve its intended dataset-specific setting.

\subsection{Implementation details}
\label{subsec:training}

All experiments are executed in Kaggle notebooks using one NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU with 16~GB memory. BCI IV-2a and BCI IV-2b use a mini-batch size of 48 and a maximum of 125 epochs. Zhou2016 uses a mini-batch size of 32 and 100 epochs. The BCI Competition runs use Adam with an initial learning rate of $9\times10^{-4}$, $\beta_1=0.5$, and weight decay $10^{-3}$; the Zhou2016 setting uses an initial learning rate of $10^{-3}$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$. A linear warm-up over the first three LOSO epochs is followed by cosine decay. The random seed (0), data split, and initialization procedure are fixed and shared by all methods and ablation variants, so that every reported comparison differs only in the evaluated component.

\subsection{Evaluation measures and reporting}
\label{subsec:metrics}

Accuracy and Cohen's $\kappa$ ($\times100$) are reported per target subject and as mean $\pm$ standard deviation across LOSO folds. Paired accuracy differences use a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test with exact sign-permutation $p$-values. Efficiency is summarized by trainable parameters, mean Stage~1 training time per fold on a Tesla T4, and batch-one CPU response time averaged over 500 passes after 100 warm-up passes; IM-TTA, preprocessing, and data transfer are excluded from these timings.

\subsection{Baseline comparison policy}
\label{subsec:baselines}

All methods follow the same LOSO folds, chronological split, and target-data access in Table~\ref{tab:protocol}; results obtained under incompatible protocols are not copied. EEG Conformer \cite{song2023eegconformer}, ATCNet \cite{altaheri2023atcnet}, TCFormer \cite{altaheri2025tcformer}, DAN \cite{long2015dan}, and CDAN \cite{tang2020cdan} are the selected baselines. The proposed method is reported in three variants:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ours-S}: source-only training with $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}$;
    \item \textbf{Ours-U}: Stage~1 UDA, including AutoDIAL, without IM-TTA; the AutoDIAL target statistics are still re-estimated on unlabeled $\mathcal{T}_e$; and
    \item \textbf{Ours}: Ours-U followed by label-free IM-TTA on the evaluation-session EEG.
\end{itemize}
Thus, Ours-U--Ours-S isolates Stage~1 adaptation, while Ours--Ours-U isolates IM-TTA.

All Zhou2016 runs of the proposed method therefore use a loop-hoisted scan: $\exp(\Delta_t A)$, $\Delta_t B_t\odot u_t$, and the $C_t$ readout are computed for all time steps outside the recurrence, leaving one multiply--add per step, and the causal depthwise convolution is evaluated as shifted multiply--adds. \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results show that adaptation is dataset dependent. The complete framework was best on BCI IV-2a and Zhou2016, whereas on BCI IV-2b the no-IM variant was slightly better and all variants were statistically indistinguishable. On Zhou2016, the complete configuration also had the lowest variability across targets, but its margins over the variants without RA, IM-TTA, or Stage~1 DA were small and cannot be significant with four folds. Filtering to 8--30~Hz and excluding the first 0.5~s focus covariance estimation on MI-related activity rather than broadband drift and cue transients; repeated-seed experiments are still required to establish the stability of these differences.

Architecturally, multi-scale and depthwise convolutions preserve local spectral--spatial evidence, while alternating selective scans and the TCN model complementary long- and short-range dynamics. The alternating scans exploit complete offline trials and are non-causal; online use would require forward-only processing and device-specific latency validation.

The method is transductive because IM-TTA accesses unlabeled evaluation-session signals before scoring. Comparisons must therefore provide baselines with identical target access. Other limitations include the three-channel BCI IV-2b setting, the small Zhou2016 cohort, and the absence of repeated-seed and fully matched baseline results. Participant sex is reported only in aggregate or not at all for the public datasets, and the cohorts are too small for sex- or gender-stratified analysis; potential sex-related differences in decoding performance were therefore not assessed and limit the generalizability of the findings.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a cross-subject MI-EEG framework combining multi-scale spatial--temporal convolution, alternating selective state-space blocks, local--global TCN fusion, hierarchical UDA, and constrained IM-TTA. Its chronological LOSO protocol excludes target labels from preprocessing, training, adaptation, and checkpoint selection. Ours obtained $75.81\pm10.63\%$ session-2 accuracy on BCI IV-2a and $83.41\pm10.35\%$ sessions~4--5 accuracy on BCI IV-2b, with corresponding Cohen's $\kappa$ scores of $67.70\pm14.20$ and $66.80\pm20.70$ on the $\times100$ scale. On Zhou2016, Ours obtained $84.33\pm0.75\%$ target-session-3 accuracy and $76.50\pm1.12$ Cohen's $\kappa$. Matched baselines and repeated-seed experiments remain necessary before drawing broader conclusions.
